# Launch strategy — domain knowledge

## Positioning discipline

- One positioning sentence per cohort, tested with real cohort members:
  - Developers (M1): lead with the tool ("agentic backlog triage that
    turns your issue tracker into a dependency-aware needs graph") —
    never with the movement.
  - Maintainers/sustainability world (M2): lead with their problem ("the
    index of the most foundational, least-supported software — and a way
    to point capacity at it").
  - Agent-owners (M3): lead with meaning + proof ("your idle agent
    capacity, pointed at work the whole ecosystem needs, credited on a
    public ledger").
  - Funders (M5): lead with mechanism credibility ("money in, attached to
    needs; settled to contributors by an auditable rule").
- The manifesto itself is the only asset that leads with the vision. That
  is its job; nothing else gets to.
- Anticipated objections — write the answers before launch, in the FAQ,
  honestly: "another crypto thing?" (no token, no trading, ledger ≠
  chain — say it in the first screen); "AI slop PRs?" (consent-first, the
  quality bar, human accountability — have `agent-engineer`'s rules
  public); "yet another bounty platform?" (bounties price *tasks*; this
  prices *the commons* — the subsidy signal is the differentiator);
  "one-human-one-voice is naive?" (point at the threat-model table; name
  the open questions as open).

## M0 — the manifesto launch

- Sequence: warm circle first (20–50 people whose engagement is likely and
  whose names carry weight in OSS-sustainability; ask for reading +
  candid response, not amplification) → then public: HN (Show HN or plain
  link; text posts framing "I'm building X because Y" outperform bare
  links for manifestos), Lobsters, relevant newsletters (Changelog,
  Console, Pointer, TLDR), SustainOSS forum, fediverse/Bluesky OSS
  circles.
- HN mechanics: weekday morning US-Eastern; founder present in-thread all
  day answering steelmanned skepticism; never argue, concede named open
  questions gladly — the manifesto's credibility asset *is* its honesty
  about open problems.
- Success metric per the protocol: not traffic — count aligned inbound
  (people who email/DM wanting to contribute) and first external ledger
  entries. 50 of the right readers > 50k drive-bys.

## M2 — the index launch (the press moment)

- This is a *data journalism* story, not a product launch. Package: the
  top-20 list, the xz/log4j backtest ("our index would have flagged xz N
  months early"), per-item evidence dossiers, the methodology, and a
  quotable maintainer or two (via `community-builder`'s pre-contact).
- Pre-brief under embargo: journalists who cover OSS sustainability (the
  beat exists: outlets that covered xz, Log4Shell, npm incidents), plus
  newsletter authors. Offer exclusives on angles, not on the data (data
  is public day one — forkability is the brand).
- Time the launch to nothing: never compete with major-conference weeks;
  and keep a fast-follow plan for the *next* xz-style incident, when the
  index becomes the "told you so" citation — that moment, not launch day,
  is when the index enters the canon.

## M3 — the proof moment

- The story is one PR, told well: the graph priced it, an outside agent
  built it, verification was credited, the maintainer is happy and says
  so. Long-form write-up with the full audit trail linked; every claim
  clickable into the glass house.
- This is the launch where skeptics are converted or lost: the
  agent-hostile OSS audience will inspect the PR itself. It must survive
  expert scrutiny before the story ships (gate with `agent-engineer`).

## Between launches — build-in-public

- One canonical channel (blog/newsletter), weekly-or-honest cadence
  (skipping beats filler; announce the cadence you'll keep). Auto-
  generated ledger digests make consistent content nearly free.
- Everything mirrors out (HN occasionally, fediverse, Bluesky) but links
  home. No engagement-bait; the audience this project needs is allergic
  to it.
- Keep a public "changelog of decisions" — decision records are content,
  and they compound into the trust that M5 asks for.

## Brand hygiene

- Visual/verbal identity must not pattern-match crypto (with
  `product-designer`): no tokenomics vocabulary, no dark-mode-neon-
  gradient aesthetic, no "community" as euphemism for holders. The
  no-token stance is a *recruiting asset* for disillusioned public-goods
  people — state it early and often.
