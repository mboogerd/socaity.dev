## 1. Why this test exists

M0 ships the manifesto, the roadmap-as-graph and the ledger and nothing else
for months, to strangers, with no track record to appeal to — comprehension
*is* the product surface. The failure mode being tested: the no-token /
retroactive-claim framing either lands as honest bookkeeping or pattern-matches
a crypto pre-mine, an airdrop farm, or unpaid-labour extraction. Cheap to
discover with five people; expensive to discover after launch.

This is ship gate #8 of the socaity-xuz resolution. The council's standing
instruction on failure is binding:

> If it fails, redesign the panel — don't add copy on top.

## 2. Recruitment

**Target: 5 participants.** Five is the number the milestone commits to; it is
enough to catch a framing that reads as a scam, and it is not a sample for
anything quantitative. Do not report percentages beyond the raw counts.

### Required criteria (all must hold)

1. **Developer.** Writes or ships software professionally or seriously as a
   hobbyist. Reads GitHub without being taught what it is. This is the M0
   audience; testing non-developers answers a different question.
2. **Zero prior exposure.** Has never read socaity.dev, the vision document,
   the repo, any council file, or any build-in-public post about the project.
   Has not heard the project explained by anyone.
3. **Not a friend of the project.** Not a personal friend, colleague, family
   member, former co-worker, or anyone with a reason to be kind. Social debt
   is the single largest threat to this test's validity — a friendly
   participant will supply the charitable reading the test is designed to
   detect the absence of.
4. **No prior working relationship with the founder.** Including online
   communities where the founder is a known figure.

### Desirable spread (not gating)

- At least 2 participants with meaningful crypto exposure (used a wallet,
  followed a token launch) — they are the population primed to detect the
  pre-mine pattern, and they generate the highest-signal red flags.
- At least 2 with none — they test whether the ledger framing is legible
  without the crypto priming that the vocabulary is written to avoid.
- At least 1 who has maintained or meaningfully contributed to an open-source
  project — closest to the M2/M3 constituency.

### Exclusions

- Anyone the founder has already pitched, in any format, ever.
- Anyone who has seen a screenshot of the /ledger page.
- Anyone participating as a favour rather than for the incentive.

### Incentive and consent

- Pay a flat, ordinary rate for ~40 minutes. Pay it regardless of what they
  say, and tell them so at the start. Unpaid participation selects for people
  who want to be helpful, which is the bias being controlled for.
- Record only with explicit consent. Session recordings are internal working
  material; do not publish raw recordings.
- Verbatim quotes may be published in aggregate findings only with consent and
  only pseudonymously (P1–P5).

### Screener script (verbatim)

> "I'm recruiting developers to look at a website for about 40 minutes and
> say what they think it is. You'll be paid regardless of your opinion, and
> critical reactions are as useful as positive ones. Three quick questions:
> Do you write software? Have you heard of a project called socaity or
> socaity.dev? Do you know anyone working on it?"

Any "yes" to the last two disqualifies. Do not describe the project, the
domain, the ledger, or the funding model during recruitment. If a candidate
asks what it's about, say: "That's exactly what I'm trying to find out from
you — I'd bias the result by telling you."

## 3. Session structure (~40 minutes)

Run one facilitator, one silent notetaker if available. Sessions are
individual — never paired or grouped; a second participant supplies the
charitable reading.

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Setup and consent | 3 min | Recording consent, payment confirmation, think-aloud briefing |
| 1. Unguided think-aloud | 10 min | Participant explores the live M0 site freely |
| 2. The three questions | 10 min | Asked verbatim, in order, in their own words |
| 3. Ledger instrumented tasks | 10 min | Three timed findability tasks on /ledger |
| 4. Open debrief | 5 min | Unstructured; catch anything the script missed |

### Phase 0 — Setup and briefing (verbatim)

> "Thanks for doing this. I'm going to show you a website. I want you to look
> around for about ten minutes and say out loud whatever you're thinking —
> what you're reading, what you expect, what confuses you, what you think of
> it. There are no wrong reactions. I didn't design this and I'm not here to
> defend it, so please be blunt. I won't answer questions while you're
> looking, because your confusion is the thing I'm here to observe. If you go
> quiet for a while I'll just say 'what are you thinking?' — that's all."

The "I didn't design this" line matters. If the founder facilitates, the test
is compromised: participants will not say "this looks like a scam" to the
person who built it. **Where possible, the founder must not facilitate.** If
the founder is the only available facilitator, they must not identify
themselves as the author, and this limitation must be recorded as a caveat on
every session sheet and in the roll-up.

### Phase 1 — Unguided exploration (10 min, no intervention)

- Start the participant on the site's landing page, logged out, on a normal
  desktop browser. Do not deep-link them to /ledger.
- Do not steer, do not answer questions, do not correct misreadings. The only
  permitted facilitator utterances are: *"What are you thinking?"* /
  *"Say more about that?"* / *"What made you say that?"*
- Never ask a leading question. Banned during phase 1: "Does that seem
  trustworthy?", "Is that clear?", "Do you understand the ledger?".
- Note where they go, in what order, where they stall, where they scroll back,
  what they skip, and what they say aloud when they hit the founder-share
  number. Note the timestamp of the first time they reach /ledger, if at all.
- If they finish early and say they're done, ask "anything else you'd look at
  before deciding whether this is for you?" and then let the clock run out.

### Phase 2 — The three questions (verbatim, in this order)

Ask each question exactly as written. Do not paraphrase, do not offer
multiple-choice, do not supply vocabulary. Capture the answer as close to
verbatim as possible; the scoring rubric operates on their words.

> **Q1. "In your own words — what is this?"**
>
> **Q2. "Who is it for?"**
>
> **Q3. "What's the catch?"**

Rules:

- Ask, then stay silent. Silence is the instrument. Do not fill a pause.
- One neutral probe per question is permitted after they finish their first
  answer: *"Anything else?"* — nothing more specific.
- Do not confirm or deny any answer, including a correct one. Nodding at Q1
  contaminates Q3.
- Q3 is the highest-value question and the most fragile. Do not soften it into
  "is there a catch?" — the presupposition is deliberate; it gives permission
  to voice suspicion, which is exactly what needs to surface here rather than
  in a public thread after launch.
- If a participant asks "what do you mean by catch?", say: *"Whatever that
  word means to you."* Nothing else.

### Phase 3 — Ledger instrumented tasks (timed)

Navigate the participant to /ledger. These three tasks are the ledger's
30-second auditability standard from the product-designer charter and
socaity-xuz screens 1–3.

Start the timer when you finish reading the prompt. Stop it when they state an
answer or point at the answer. **Cap each task at 60 seconds** and record the
outcome as a miss past 30 seconds — do not let them grind, and do not help.

| # | Prompt (verbatim) | Target | Maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "Show me who has contributed what." | ≤30s | xuz screen 1 — contribution table |
| T2 | "Find the rule that turns a contribution into a claim." | ≤30s | xuz screen 2 — rate card / V |
| T3 | "Work out what your own contribution would be worth." | ≤30s | xuz screen 3 — calculator |

Record for each: time to answer, found / found-late (30–60s) / not found, the
path they took, and any wrong turn. A wrong-first-click that self-corrects
inside 30 seconds is still a pass, but log the wrong click — it is design
signal for socaity-xuz.

After T3, ask one follow-up: *"What does that number mean to you?"* This is a
diagnostic, not a timed task. If the participant reads the calculator output as
money owed, as a balance, or as something tradable, that is a socaity-uun
finding regardless of how the three questions scored.

### Phase 4 — Open debrief (5 min)

Permitted here and only here:

- "Was there anything on the site you didn't believe?"
- "What does the person who built this get out of it?" *(mandatory — see red
  flags)*
- "If a friend sent you this link, what would you say back to them?"
- "Is there anything you'd want to know before contributing?"

Then thank them, pay them, and answer any questions they have about the
project honestly and at length. The debrief is where the honesty debt is
repaid; do not skip it.

## 4. Success bar

The bar is **4 of 5 participants** giving a *correct* answer on **each** of the
three questions **and on the mechanism check (M)**, scored against
`scoring-rubric.md`. Correctness is judged on substance in their own words —
vocabulary matching is explicitly not required and matching our vocabulary
without the concept scores as incorrect.

The answers that count as correct in outline:

- **Q1 — What is this?** A platform for funding / directing work on shared
  software infrastructure — the commons. Not a job board, not a crypto
  project, not a donation site.
- **Q2 — Who is it for?** Developers and the people who depend on open-source
  infrastructure — contributors on one side, the projects and funders who
  need under-supported work done on the other.
- **Q3 — What's the catch?** *Contribution is recorded now and paid later,
  only if money ever arrives.* Stated as a **risk they can articulate**, not as
  a scam accusation. The distinction is the whole test: "I might do work and
  never get paid, because that depends on funding that doesn't exist yet" is a
  pass. "It's a scheme to get free labour" is a fail.
- **M — the mechanism check.** Not a fourth question. Scored from what the
  participant already said across phases 1–4 (typically inside Q1, Q3, and the
  T3 follow-up), never by asking them a leading question about it. Three
  elements, all required:
  **(i) no payment now** — nothing is paid out today, and nothing is bought,
  sold, or issued as a tradable instrument (**no token**);
  **(ii) the record** — contribution is written down on a ledger that anyone
  can look at;
  **(iii) the retroactive claim** — that record is what a later payout is
  divided by, if there ever is one.
  This is the milestone's own bar, and it is what separates a participant who
  understood the deal from one who merely felt fine about it.

Each item is scored independently: 4/5 on Q1, 4/5 on Q2, 4/5 on Q3, and 4/5
on M. A test where Q1 and Q2 score 5/5 and Q3 scores 2/5 **fails** — Q3 is the
question the test exists for and does not average away. The same holds for M.

### Secondary bars (recorded, non-gating on their own)

- **Ledger tasks:** 4/5 participants complete each of T1, T2, T3 within 30
  seconds. Failure here is a /ledger information-design finding for
  socaity-xuz, not necessarily a launch blocker — unless it co-occurs with a
  Q3 failure, in which case treat it as one: if they can't find the rule, the
  "catch" answer isn't grounded in anything.
- **The founder-share read (xuz ship gate):** on encountering the concentration
  panel, the participant states **why** the share is 100% ("he's the only one
  so far") rather than **what** ("he kept it all"). Record verbatim. The
  council set this as the pass/fail line for the panel specifically.

## 5. Launch-blocking red flags

These are **individually blocking**. One participant is enough. They are not
scored on a 4/5 basis, because the failure they represent is qualitative: if
one cold developer in five reaches for these, a public launch reaches a
population where many will.

**RF1 — Unprompted "crypto", "airdrop", "token", "pyramid", "ponzi",
"pre-mine", or "NFT" about the ledger or the epoch-share mechanism.**
"Unprompted" means the participant introduces the word; a facilitator must
never use any of these words in a session. Blocking whether the participant
frames it as an accusation, a joke, or a neutral comparison ("oh, it's like an
airdrop") — the pattern-match is the failure, and a neutral one is worse
signal than a hostile one because it means the framing reads as crypto to
people who aren't even suspicious.

**RF2 — Cannot say what the founder gets out of it.** Asked directly in phase
4. "I don't know", "that's what I can't figure out", or an answer that is
wrong in a self-dealing direction ("he keeps most of it", "he cashes out
first") all trigger. An unexplained founder incentive is the vacuum every scam
suspicion fills.

**RF3 — Reads the recorded contribution as an obligation on them.** Any belief
that contributing costs money, requires a purchase, buys in, or that they
would be locked in. Indicates the ledger reads as an investment product,
which is both a comprehension failure and a legal-exposure signal (route to
legal-counsel as well as socaity-xuz).

**RF4 — Reads the founder share as taking rather than as an artefact of N=1.**
"He kept it all", "he gave himself everything", "the founder owns 100%" with
no grasp that there is exactly one contributor. This is the xuz panel's
explicit pass/fail line.

**RF5 — Cannot answer Q1 at all** — "I genuinely don't know what this is"
after ten minutes on the site. Not a nuance failure; the manifesto has not
communicated its subject.

### What a red flag does

A red flag **blocks launch until the surface that caused it is redesigned and
the affected participants' path is re-tested**. It does not mandate a full
five-person re-run in every case — see §7 — but it does mandate a recorded
decision, a change, and evidence that the change works.

Explicitly *not* an acceptable response to any red flag: adding explanatory
copy on top of the surface that failed. The socaity-xuz resolution forbids it
by name. If a stranger reads "he kept it all", the panel is redesigned.

## 6. Feeding failures back

Every finding is routed, not filed. Two destinations:

### → socaity-xuz — /ledger presentation

Route here anything about **what the page shows and in what order**:

- T1/T2/T3 misses or slow finds, and the wrong turns taken en route → screen
  order, table anatomy, calculator placement (xuz resolution items 2, 4, 5).
- RF4 and any "kept it all" reading → the concentration panel (item 3). The
  council's binding instruction: redesign the panel, do not add copy.
- Misreadings of the "if the epoch closed today" figure, or the calculator
  output read as money/balance/owed → item 5 calculator bindings.
- Any participant who cropped their own attention to the 100% figure without
  reading the canonical block → item 1 headline block, and re-run LS's
  1200×630 crop test on the real screenshot.
- Failure to find or open the "Is this a pre-mine?" answer before voicing the
  suspicion → item 1 placement.

Attach verbatim quotes. The council operates on what strangers actually said;
paraphrase loses the signal.

### → socaity-uun — anti-crypto vocabulary and visual standard

Route here anything about **words and visual register**:

- Every RF1 trigger, with the exact sentence and the exact screen element the
  participant was looking at when they said it. That element is a checklist
  item for the pre-publish standard.
- Any participant using "wallet", "balance", "holdings", "mining", "staking",
  "rewards" to describe what they saw — our vocabulary discipline failed
  upstream of their reading.
- Any visual element named as a crypto tell (a chart style, an icon, a number
  format, a colour treatment) → the visual half of the checklist.
- The "credit" naming question left open on socaity-uun: record verbatim how
  participants reacted to that word specifically, if it appears on any M0
  surface. Five cold readings is the evidence that question has been waiting
  for.

### Other routes

- RF3 (contribution read as a purchase/investment) → also flag to
  legal-counsel; a lay reader construing epoch shares as an investment product
  is a regulatory signal, not only a UX one.
- Q2 failures (wrong audience) → launch-strategist; that is positioning, not
  ledger design.

### Mechanics

For each finding, open or comment on the destination issue with: participant
ID, the verbatim quote, the screen/element, and the proposed change class
(redesign / reorder / rename / delete). Do not batch findings into a single
prose summary — one routed finding per defect, so each can be closed
independently and the re-test can target it.

## 7. Launch gate

**This test runs before the M0 site launches, not after.** It is ship gate #8
of the socaity-xuz resolution and the milestone's own M0 test plan. A one-week
test that runs after launch answers a question that has already been answered
publicly and irreversibly by the first hostile thread.

The gate is a **recorded decision, never an implied one.** Someone writes the
line, dates it, signs it, and commits it. "We ran the test and it seemed fine"
is not a decision; an unwritten pass is a fail.

### The decision line

At the bottom of the completed `results-template.md` roll-up, exactly one of:

- **GO** — 4/5 or better on each of Q1, Q2, Q3 and M, and zero red flags
  across all five sessions. Launch may proceed. Secondary-bar misses are filed
  as follow-up work and named in the decision line.
- **NO-GO** — any of Q1, Q2, Q3 or M below 4/5, or any red flag. Name the
  failing item(s) and/or red flag(s), the surface responsible, the routed
  issue, and the re-test scope.

There is no third option. "GO with reservations" is not available; a
reservation strong enough to name is a NO-GO with a specific fix.

### Re-test scope after a NO-GO

- **Red flag on a specific surface, questions otherwise passing:** redesign
  the surface, then run **3 fresh participants** (never the same ones — they
  are now contaminated) through phases 1–3. The red flag must not recur and
  the three questions must still hold. Record a second decision line.
- **Q1, Q2, Q3 or M below 4/5:** the framing problem is upstream of any single
  surface. Full **5-participant re-run** after the fix, all fresh recruits.
- **Ledger task misses only, questions passing, no red flags:** GO is
  permitted. File the /ledger fixes on socaity-xuz and re-test them at the
  next natural opportunity; findability is a defect, not a credibility
  failure.

### Recording

- The completed capture sheets and roll-up live in this directory, committed.
  The glass-house principle applies to our own research: a NO-GO and its
  reasons are published like anything else.
- Participant identities are never committed. P1–P5 only.
- If the founder facilitated, that caveat is recorded on the roll-up and
  carried into the decision line.
