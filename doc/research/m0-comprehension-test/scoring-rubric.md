# M0 Comprehension Test — Scoring Rubric

**Status: NOT YET APPLIED.** No sessions have been run and no answers have been
scored. Every example answer below is an *illustrative* construction written to
calibrate scorers — none is a quote from a participant, because there are no
participants yet.

Companion to [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md). Read this before facilitating, and
again before scoring.

---

## How to score

1. **Score from the verbatim answer, not from your memory of the session.**
   Write the answer down first, score second. Scoring from impression is how
   "they basically got it" enters the record.
2. **Score substance, never vocabulary.** A participant who says "you log the
   work you do and if the project ever gets money, that log decides who gets
   what" has answered Q3 correctly without using one word of our vocabulary. A
   participant who says "epoch shares on a public ledger" while believing it's
   a token has answered incorrectly while using all of it. **Borrowed
   vocabulary without the concept scores as incorrect**, and is itself a
   finding for socaity-uun: our words are being repeated rather than
   understood.
3. **Score the first answer plus one "anything else?" probe.** Anything
   volunteered after the facilitator moves on is recorded but not scored.
4. **Two scorers where possible**, independently, then reconcile. Where they
   disagree and cannot reconcile, take the **lower** score. The test exists to
   find failure; charitable tie-breaking defeats it.
5. **Partial is not a pass.** The 4/5 bar counts *correct* only. Partials are
   recorded and routed as findings but never counted toward the bar.
6. **Red flags are scored separately and independently of question scores.** A
   participant can answer all three questions correctly and still trigger a
   blocking red flag. That combination is a NO-GO. Do not let a good
   question score suppress a red flag — the participant who says "it's a
   commons funding thing, basically an airdrop for maintainers" understood the
   model *and* pattern-matched it to crypto, and the second fact is the one
   that blocks launch.

## Q1 — "In your own words, what is this?"

**Concept required:** a platform for funding or directing work on shared
software infrastructure — the commons — where needs are made visible and
contributions to them are recorded.

The load-bearing elements: (a) it is about **software that many people depend
on / open source**, and (b) it is about **getting work on that software funded,
prioritised, or done**. Both required for correct.

### Correct

The answer names software-commons work *and* the funding/direction of it. It
may be vague about mechanism; it may be wrong about details. It must be right
about the subject.

*Illustrative examples of answers that score correct:*

- "It's trying to fix the thing where everyone's stack depends on some library
  one guy maintains for free. It maps out what needs doing and keeps a record
  of who does it so they can get paid eventually."
- "A system for figuring out which open-source projects are important and
  under-funded, and then organising people to work on them."
- "Kind of a public to-do list for open source, plus a ledger that tracks who
  did what so money can be split later."
- "A platform where the demand for infrastructure work gets written down, and
  the work people do against it is logged publicly."

### Partial

One of the two elements present, or the subject right but the purpose
misread. Record, route, do not count.

*Illustrative examples of answers that score partial:*

- "It's a manifesto about open-source sustainability." — right domain, but
  reads it as a piece of writing rather than a platform. Common and forgivable
  at M0 when only a document exists; still a partial.
- "Some kind of public log of work someone's doing." — the ledger registered,
  the commons purpose did not.
- "It's an open-source project tracker." — right shape, missing the funding /
  commons dimension entirely.
- "A way to pay open-source maintainers." — funding present, but reads it as a
  donation platform (see the note below).

### Incorrect

Wrong category. Includes any answer whose head noun is a crypto or finance
product, and any answer that cannot locate the subject.

*Illustrative examples of answers that score incorrect:*

- "It's a crypto project — some kind of token for open source." *(also RF1)*
- "A freelance marketplace / job board for developers."
- "A donation site, like Patreon or Open Collective for repos." — this is the
  **most likely near-miss**, and it is incorrect, not partial: it inverts the
  model. Donations are gifts to a chosen recipient; this prices work against
  demand and records a claim. If two or more participants land here, that is
  a positioning finding for launch-strategist and a manifesto finding, not a
  ledger one.
- "An AI agent thing? Something about agents doing pull requests." — M3 read
  as the whole product.
- "Honestly, I don't know." *(also RF5)*

## Q2 — "Who is it for?"

**Concept required:** developers, and the open-source projects / people who
depend on under-supported infrastructure. Correct answers name a **supply
side** (people who could do the work) or a **demand side** (projects,
maintainers, or the organisations depending on them) with a plausible reason.

Naming only one side is sufficient for correct **if the side named is right and
reasoned**. This question is easier than Q1 and Q3 by design; it is a check
that the audience isn't badly mis-set, not a deep probe.

### Correct

*Illustrative examples:*

- "Developers. Specifically people who care about open source and are annoyed
  that the maintenance work is invisible."
- "Maintainers of libraries nobody funds — and I guess also companies that
  depend on those libraries and don't know it."
- "People who'd contribute to open source if there were any chance of getting
  credit or money for it."
- "Right now? Early people who like the idea enough to be first. Later,
  regular devs."  — audience-over-time reading; correct.

### Partial

Right population, no reasoning, or an audience so broad it carries no
information.

*Illustrative examples:*

- "Developers." *(with nothing further after the probe)* — technically right,
  demonstrates nothing.
- "Open-source people." — same.
- "Companies, I think?" — plausible demand side, but stated as a guess with no
  connection to anything on the site.

### Incorrect

Wrong population, or an audience defined by financial motive.

*Illustrative examples:*

- "Investors. People looking to get in early." *(strong signal; check for RF1
  and RF3)*
- "Crypto people."
- "Everyone / the general public."
- "The founder, mostly." *(cynical read; record verbatim and check RF4 —
  distinguish 'this only benefits him' from a correct read that the ledger
  currently only contains him)*
- "Non-technical people who want software built."

## Q3 — "What's the catch?"

**This is the question the test exists for.** It carries the launch decision.

**Concept required, in three parts:**

1. **Deferral** — you contribute now and are not paid now.
2. **Conditionality** — payment happens only if money ever arrives; it is not
   guaranteed.
3. **Register: risk, not fraud** — the participant frames this as a bet they
   could choose to take, an uncertainty, a "why I might not bother" — *not* as
   a deception being run on them.

All three required for correct. Part 3 is not a formality: the difference
between "I could do a bunch of work and get nothing, because the money might
never show up" and "so they get free labour and you get an IOU" is the entire
result. The first is a person who understood the deal. The second is a person
who believes there is a mark.

### Correct

*Illustrative examples:*

- "The catch is you're working now for a promise. If money never comes in, the
  ledger is just a list of stuff you did for free. That's a real risk and it's
  on the page, so — fine, but it's a bet."
- "You get paid later and only if there's anything to pay out of. Nobody's
  hiding that, but it means the whole thing depends on them actually raising
  money at some point."
- "There's no money yet. You'd be contributing on the expectation that a
  future pot gets split according to this record. Whether that pot ever exists
  is the open question."
- "Honestly the catch is it might just not work. Not that it's dishonest —
  it's that there's one guy and no revenue, so the claim you're accruing might
  be worth nothing."
- "You're trusting that the rule doesn't change and that money shows up. The
  rule's public which helps, but I'd want to see a second contributor."

Note the last two: **scepticism about viability is correct**, and is exactly
the register the framing is designed to produce. Do not score down for
doubt — score down for suspicion of bad faith.

### Partial

Deferral or conditionality present but incomplete, or the register ambiguous
after one probe.

*Illustrative examples:*

- "You don't get paid up front." — deferral only; no conditionality. Probe
  once ("anything else?"); if it doesn't develop, partial.
- "There's some rule about how it's split that I didn't fully follow." —
  registered that a conversion exists, didn't get the conditionality.
- "I'm not sure — it seems very upfront about everything, so maybe there
  isn't one?" — the "no catch" answer. Partial, not correct: the deal has a
  real risk and the participant hasn't located it. Also worth routing: a
  page so reassuring that the risk disappears is its own failure, and one the
  honesty rails are supposed to prevent.
- "The catch is it's early and nothing works yet." — true, but a
  maturity observation, not the deal's structure.

### Incorrect

Any answer whose register is fraud, extraction, or a crypto pattern-match —
regardless of how mechanically accurate the rest of it is.

*Illustrative examples:*

- "The catch is it's free labour. You work, he owns the record, you get
  nothing." *(check RF2, RF4)*
- "Classic — early contributors get 'shares' that turn into a token later."
  *(RF1)*
- "It's a pyramid. First in does best." *(RF1)*
- "You have to buy in, right? Or stake something?" *(RF1, RF3)*
- "The catch is he already gave himself 100% of it." *(RF4 — and note this
  answer can be *mechanically* aware and still fail: the participant sees the
  concentration and reads it as taking. This is precisely the socaity-xuz
  panel pass/fail line.)*
- "I couldn't tell you. I didn't understand what you'd be signing up for."

### Boundary calls on Q3 (read before scoring)

- **"It's like an airdrop, but for work" — said neutrally, even
  approvingly.** Score **incorrect**, and trigger RF1. The comparison is the
  failure; a friendly crypto pattern-match still means the framing reads as
  crypto.
- **"I'd want to know it's not a scam, but reading this, it doesn't look like
  one."** Score **correct** if parts 1 and 2 are present. Considering and
  *rejecting* the scam hypothesis on the evidence is the framing working, not
  failing. Do not trigger RF1 unless a flagged word was used about the
  mechanism itself.
- **"The catch is I'd have to trust one guy."** Correct if paired with
  deferral/conditionality; the single-founder concentration risk is a true and
  disclosed feature, and the page names it ("what this page can't prove yet").
- **A participant who asks the facilitator "is there a catch?" back.** Do not
  answer. Say "whatever that word means to you" and wait. If they cannot
  produce anything, score incorrect and check RF5.

## M — the mechanism check (no fourth question is asked)

**This is the milestone's own success-bar item and it gates launch at 4/5,
exactly like Q1–Q3.** It is *not* asked. Score it from what the participant
already volunteered across phases 1–4 — usually inside their Q1 and Q3 answers
and the T3 follow-up *"what does that number mean to you?"*. Never prompt for
it; a mechanism the participant only produces when asked about it directly is
a mechanism the site failed to convey, and prompting destroys the measurement.

**Three elements, all required for correct:**

| # | Element | Met when the participant's own words convey… | Not met when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| i | **No payment now / no token** | nothing is paid out today, and nothing is bought, sold, held, or issued as a tradable thing | they think there is a coin, a share you can sell, a payout on contribution, or something you buy (also check RF1, RF3) |
| ii | **The record** | contribution is written down somewhere anyone can go and look at | they think the record is private, internal, or the founder's own accounting |
| iii | **The retroactive claim** | that record is what a future payout would be divided by, if one ever happens | they think payment is a rate, a salary, a bounty per task, or a promise of a fixed amount |

### Correct

All three, in any words, anywhere in the session.

*Illustrative constructions (not participant quotes):*

- "Nothing's paid now. It just logs what you did, publicly, and if money turns
  up later that log is how it gets divided."
- "There's no coin or anything — it's a list. The list decides the split if
  there's ever a split."

### Partial

One or two elements present. Record which; the missing element names the
surface that failed. Missing (i) routes to socaity-uun; missing (ii) or (iii)
routes to socaity-xuz.

*Illustrative constructions:*

- "It records what you contribute." — (ii) only; no deferral, no claim.
- "You get paid later out of whatever comes in." — (i) and (iii); no grasp
  that the record is public and is the instrument.

### Incorrect

Zero elements, or any element replaced by a token/tradable-instrument reading.

*Illustrative constructions:*

- "You accrue shares you can presumably sell later." *(RF1)*
- "It's an hourly rate you invoice for once they're funded."
- "I didn't work out what actually happens with the numbers."

**Overlap with Q3 is expected and is not double-counting.** Q3 scores the
*register* (risk vs fraud); M scores the *mechanics*. A participant can be
correct on Q3 and incorrect on M — "you might never get paid, who knows how
it works" is a well-calibrated risk read with no mechanism — and that
combination is a NO-GO on M.

## Red flag determination

Red flags are recorded per participant as triggered / not triggered, with the
verbatim sentence and the screen element in view. See PROTOCOL.md §5 for the
definitions and their consequence. Scoring notes:

| Flag | Trigger test | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|
| RF1 | Participant introduces crypto/airdrop/token/pyramid/ponzi/pre-mine/NFT **about the ledger or epoch shares** | Participant says "unlike crypto, this…" in a *contrast* they construct after reading the register line — **not** a trigger; the page's own framing produced it. Record it as positive evidence. |
| RF2 | Phase-4 answer to "what does the person who built this get out of it?" is absent, or self-dealing | "He gets paid for his own work at the same rate as anyone, and he gets the thing built" is a **correct** answer, not a trigger. |
| RF3 | Believes contributing costs money, requires purchase, or locks them in | "I'd be investing my time" is a metaphor, not a trigger. "I'd have to put money in" is. |
| RF4 | Reads founder share as taking, not as N=1 | "He's got 100% because he's the only one — obviously" is the **pass** state. "He's got 100%" with no *why* after a probe is the trigger. |
| RF5 | Cannot answer Q1 at all after 10 minutes | A participant who answers Q1 partially is not RF5. |

When in doubt on a red flag, **trigger it**. A false red flag costs a redesign
and three re-test participants. A missed one costs the launch.
